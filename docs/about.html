About Tender Reach

Procurement shouldn't require an insider to understand it.

Most tender notices are written for people who already know the system. Tender Reach exists for everyone else — the supplier who's never had someone walk them through it before.

Why we exist

Zimbabwe's Procurement Regulatory Authority (PRAZ) runs a real, functioning electronic procurement system — tenders, awards, and procurement plans are all published there. But publishing information isn't the same as making it usable. Dense regulatory language, no plain-language guidance, and no alerts mean smaller and rural suppliers often miss opportunities entirely, or lose out simply because they didn't understand what was being asked of them.

Tender Reach doesn't replace or compete with PRAZ's system. We built a companion layer on top of what's already public — one that speaks the way a helpful, knowledgeable friend would, not the way a regulation does.

How Tender Reach actually works

Everything happens over WhatsApp, because that's where suppliers already are — no new app to download, no login to remember.

1. Ask a procurement question

Not sure how to register as a supplier, or what a specific clause means? Ask in plain language. We're building toward instant AI-assisted answers; today, a real person on our team responds within 24 hours.

2. Get a tender explained

Forward a tender notice you've found — from PRAZ's portal, the Government Gazette, or anywhere else — and we'll return a plain-language summary: what's required, the deadline, and a document checklist, without the legal phrasing getting in the way.

3. Learn what we're building

Tender discovery, document guidance, supplier support, and AI-assisted answers — all aimed at one goal: making procurement clear, fair, and accessible for every business in Zimbabwe, not just the ones who already know the ropes.

The Founding Supplier Programme

Before building more, we want to understand real procurement experience from real suppliers. That's what the Founding Supplier Programme is for.

What joining involves

A short, conversational interview on WhatsApp — about 8–10 minutes — covering how you currently find tenders, what's hardest about applying, and what one thing would help you most. It's a conversation, not a form: you can stop at any point, and whatever you've shared up to then still helps shape what we build.

What you get

  • Free, priority access to plain-language tender summaries and deadline reminders — no paywall, ever, for this core support.
  • Direct input into what Tender Reach builds next. Your answers shape our actual product roadmap, not just a feedback form nobody reads.
  • Recognition as a founding member once we launch publicly, only if you choose to be named.

What we ask in return

Just honesty. We're not looking for suppliers who've had a perfect experience — we're looking to understand where the system is genuinely hard to navigate, so we can actually fix it.

Procurement Knowledge Centre

How public procurement works, briefly

Public bodies in Zimbabwe — government ministries, parastatals, local authorities — buy goods, works, and services through competitive tenders rather than picking suppliers directly. The Procurement Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (PRAZ) oversees this process under the Public Procurement and Disposal of Public Assets Act. Since 2024, most of this has run through PRAZ's electronic Government Procurement (eGP) system, where tenders are published, bids submitted, and awards recorded.

In practice, this means: a tender is published with a closing date, suppliers who meet the stated requirements submit a bid before that date, and the procuring entity evaluates and awards based on criteria set out in the tender document itself. The rules are consistent — but the documents describing them often aren't written for someone outside the profession.

Making sense of a tender document

Most tender notices follow a similar shape, even when the wording is dense:

  • Reference number and category — identifies the tender and what kind of supplier it's open to.
  • Scope of requirement — what's actually being procured, in the procuring entity's own terms.
  • Eligibility and compliance requirements — registration status, tax clearance, and any category-specific conditions.
  • Submission requirements — the specific documents and format expected, and the closing date and time.
  • Evaluation criteria — how bids will be scored, which tells you what to emphasize in your submission.

This is exactly what our WhatsApp assistant is built to unpack for a specific tender you share with us — turning that structure into a plain checklist.

How our tender index stays current

Alongside the Founding Supplier Programme, we maintain a live, continuously refreshed index of public tenders from across Zimbabwe — sourced directly from PRAZ's public eGP bulletin board, the same information any visitor can already view openly on their site, just organized in one place and kept current automatically.

This index is what powers the assistant's ability to answer questions like "what tenders are open in my industry right now" or "when does this one close" — grounded in real, current listings.

What this is

  • A regularly refreshed index of tender ID, reference number, title, category, procuring entity, and publish/closing dates — all fields already publicly visible on PRAZ's bulletin board.
  • Used to power search, alerts, and assistant-guided answers inside Tender Reach.

What this isn't

  • We don't access any authenticated area of PRAZ's system, download bid documents, or touch anything behind a supplier login.
  • We don't resell this index or offer it as a standalone data product — it exists only to make Tender Reach's own guidance current.
  • We fetch politely: rate-limited, clearly identified, and not in a way designed to burden PRAZ's infrastructure.
